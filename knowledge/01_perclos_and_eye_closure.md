# PERCLOS and eye closure

PERCLOS is the proportion of time, over a moving window, that a person's eyelids are closed beyond a set fraction (a common definition uses 80% closure). It is one of the most widely used camera-based indicators of drowsiness in driver-monitoring research. Higher PERCLOS over a minute or more is associated with lower alertness.

In CONTEXTRA the eye is treated as closed when the eye aspect ratio (EAR) falls below 72% of that driver's own open-eye baseline, measured during a short calibration at start. PERCLOS here is therefore a PERCLOS-style measure, not the exact clinical definition.

A normal blink lasts roughly a tenth to four tenths of a second. A closure that lasts about one second or longer is not an ordinary blink and is treated as a long closure or possible microsleep. Eye closure is an indicator of drowsiness, not a diagnosis: glasses, lighting, squinting or looking down can also reduce the measured eye opening.
